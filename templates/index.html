Frequently Asked Questions

Vertical property records and spatial verification

Clear answers for citizens, survey teams, and officers using the property mapping workflow.

What problem does this system solve?

It makes ownership and boundaries easier to understand in multi-storey properties, where a single flat land record cannot clearly show separate floors, units, and shared spaces.

What is a Vertical ULPIN?

It is a unique property identity for a specific vertical unit, such as a floor, apartment, office, or defined shared area within a building.

What does ULPIN stand for?

ULPIN stands for Unique Land Parcel Identification Number. In this workflow, the idea is extended so that a distinct vertical unit can also be precisely located within the parcel and building.

What is a vertical property?

It is a property where rights are connected to space above the land, such as an apartment, a floor in an office tower, a basement parking level, a terrace right, or a shared corridor.

What is the difference between a parcel, a building, a floor, and a unit?

A parcel is the defined piece of land. A building sits on that parcel. A floor is one level of the building. A unit is a specific usable or owned space within a floor, such as a flat, office, shop, or storage area.

Why is a plot number alone not enough for a tall building?

A plot number identifies the land, but it does not distinguish who owns or uses each level above it. Vertical mapping connects each registered unit to its exact place in the building.

What are common areas in a vertical property?

Common areas are spaces used jointly by eligible occupants, such as lobbies, staircases, lifts, corridors, utility rooms, parking access, gardens, and shared terraces. Their rights need to be recorded separately from exclusive units.

Can one building have more than one owner?

Yes. Different people or organisations may hold rights over separate apartments, offices, floors, parking spaces, or commercial units, while the land and common areas may have shared or separately defined interests.

What information is connected to a mapped unit?

A record can connect the unit identity with its parcel, floor, usage, area, spatial position, registration details, verification status, and the documents used during review.

Does a Vertical ULPIN itself prove legal ownership?

No. It is a precise spatial identity that supports verification and record keeping. Legal ownership is determined through the applicable registration, title, and approval process.

Can a unit change hands without changing its spatial identity?

Usually, the physical unit can keep the same spatial identity when ownership changes. The ownership and registration details are updated, while the mapped location remains the reference for that unit.

Why is a 3D view useful when documents already exist?

Documents are essential, but they can be difficult to compare across floors. A 3D view shows the relationship between land, building levels, units, common spaces, and nearby boundaries in one place.

What is spatial verification?

Spatial verification compares the recorded description of a property with its mapped position, size, level, and boundaries. It helps identify information that may need review before a record is accepted.

What is the difference between a warning and a confirmed conflict?

A warning is an early signal that measurements or boundaries may need checking. A confirmed conflict is recorded only after the authorised review process examines the available survey and registration evidence.

What does an overlap warning mean?

It means two mapped boundaries or vertical units may occupy some of the same space. The warning asks for review before a record is finalised.

What kinds of overlap can occur?

Examples include two parcel boundaries crossing, a building extending beyond its recorded parcel, two units claiming the same floor area, or a shared space being incorrectly recorded as exclusive ownership.

Does an overlap warning mean someone has done something wrong?

No. It can result from older surveys, incomplete records, construction changes, data-entry differences, or an actual dispute. The warning is a reason to verify the facts, not a final judgement.

What evidence can be used to review an overlap?

Review may use approved plans, survey measurements, registration documents, title records, site photographs, local authority permissions, and a fresh field survey where required.

Who resolves an overlap or boundary conflict?

An authorised land-record or registration officer reviews the evidence and may request a field re-survey, supporting documents, or corrections from the concerned parties.

What is an on-site re-survey?

It is a fresh measurement of the property at the physical location. It can confirm boundaries, building placement, floor dimensions, and the relationship between the map and the built structure.

Can a record be corrected after it has been created?

Yes, subject to the applicable authority and procedure. A correction should preserve an audit trail showing what changed, why it changed, and the evidence used to support the update.

Why is an audit trail important?

It records important actions such as checks, corrections, officer decisions, and issued extracts. This makes the review process easier to trace and explain later.

Can this system prevent every property dispute?

No system can eliminate every dispute. Its value is in making property information clearer, exposing inconsistencies sooner, and giving reviewers a better basis for evidence-led decisions.

How does this help citizens?

It gives people a clearer view of the unit being recorded, reduces ambiguity around floor-level ownership, and helps surface disputes earlier in the process.

How does this help government officers?

It brings parcel, building, floor, verification, and risk information into one review flow, making it easier to compare records, document decisions, and request the right follow-up evidence.

What happens after a re-survey?

The corrected measurements are reviewed, the spatial record is updated where appropriate, and the verification workflow can continue with a clearer boundary reference.
